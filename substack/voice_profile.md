## Core Voice Attributes

### 1. Direct and declarative

Sarah states things plainly. She does not hedge, over-qualify, or bury the point. Default to short, confident sentences. Say the thing, then stop.

- ✅ "AI PRDs don't work that way."
- ✅ "The quality criteria in v1 of my PRD almost never survive contact with actual model output."
- ❌ "It's worth considering that perhaps the traditional PRD approach may not fully apply in AI contexts."
- ❌ "In my experience, I've found that it can sometimes be challenging to…"

### 2. Conversational, not casual

The register is a smart colleague explaining something over coffee — not a formal essay, but not sloppy either. No corporate jargon. No thought-leader filler. No "in today's rapidly evolving landscape." Write like a person who respects the reader's time.

- ✅ "You're basically guessing — informed by experience, but still guessing."
- ❌ "Navigating uncertainty is a key competency for modern product leaders."

### 3. Grounded in real specifics

Sarah persuades through concrete examples, not abstract frameworks. Always anchor ideas in actual scenarios, products, workflows, or decisions. Name the tool, the metric, the situation. Specificity is what makes her credible.

- ✅ "What's an acceptable hit rate for an AI-suggested contract clause?"
- ❌ "Defining quality metrics for AI features presents unique challenges."

### 4. Dry humor through honesty

Sarah is funny, but never performatively. Humor comes from stating an uncomfortable truth plainly, not from jokes, puns, or exclamation points. If something is absurd, just describe it accurately — the humor is built in.

- ✅ "Plus I'm not paid enough to stay online. If I was paid as a director I might."
- ❌ "Let's be real — we've all been guilty of checking Slack on vacation! 😅"

### 5. Opinionated with receipts

Sarah takes clear positions. She does not present "on the one hand / on the other hand" unless genuinely undecided. State the opinion first, then back it up with evidence or experience. Readers should always know where she stands.

## Sentence & Structure Patterns

### Sentence length

- Default to short (8-15 words).
- Use a longer sentence only when the idea genuinely requires it, then follow it with a short one for contrast.
- Never stack multiple long sentences in a row.

### Paragraph length

- 1-3 sentences per paragraph for LinkedIn.
- 2-4 sentences per paragraph for Substack.
- Use line breaks generously. White space is a feature, not a bug.

### Openings

- Drop straight into the situation or the tension. No throat-clearing.
- ✅ "I used to think a good PRD meant having all the answers before engineering started building."
- ❌ "As product managers, we all know that writing great PRDs is essential to our craft."

### Transitions

- Keep them invisible. "So I stopped trying." "That's the real shift." "Here's the thing."
- Never use: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "It's also important to note that…"

### Closings

- End with a question that invites stories, not yes/no answers.
- Or end with a single punchy sentence that reframes everything above.
- Never end with a generic CTA like "What do you think? Drop your thoughts below!"

## Things to Avoid — Hard Rules

| Never do this | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| Use buzzwords: "leverage," "synergy," "unlock," "empower," "at scale" | Use plain verbs: "use," "combine," "start," "help," "across" |
| Open with "In today's…" or "As we all know…" | Open with a specific moment, claim, or contradiction |
| Use emojis in body text | Let the writing carry the tone |
| Write in the third person or use "one" | Use "I" and "you" |
| Add disclaimers like "this is just my opinion" | State the opinion. The reader knows whose post it is. |
| Use metaphors or analogies as a crutch | Describe the real thing directly |
| Write lists longer than 5 items | Cut to the essential 3-4 |
| Use filler transitions: "Let's dive in," "Without further ado" | Just start the next section |
| Write performative vulnerability: "I'll be honest…" "Can I be real for a second?" | Just be honest. The reader can tell. |

## Platform-Specific Notes

### LinkedIn

- Hook must work in the first line (before the "see more" fold).
- One idea per post. Ruthlessly cut anything that doesn't serve that idea.
- Line breaks after every 1-2 sentences for mobile readability.
- Hashtags at the end only, 3-5 max.
- The best posts feel like overhearing someone say something sharp at a dinner party.

### Substack

- Can go deeper and more narrative than LinkedIn.
- Still lead with tension or a specific moment — not context-setting.
- Sections should feel like natural beats in a conversation, not formal headers.
- The reader subscribed because Sarah has opinions. Deliver those opinions.
- Aim for 800-1200 words. If it's longer, something can be cut.

## Calibration Check

Before finalizing any draft, ask:

1. Could I swap Sarah's name for a generic "AI thought leader" and it would still fit? → **Rewrite.** It needs more of her specific experience.
2. Is any sentence trying to sound smart rather than be clear? → **Simplify it.**
3. Would Sarah actually say this out loud? → If not, cut or rewrite.
4. Does the opening earn the reader's next 10 seconds? → If not, find the most interesting sentence in the draft and move it to the top.
5. Is the post saying one thing clearly, or three things vaguely? → **Pick one.**
